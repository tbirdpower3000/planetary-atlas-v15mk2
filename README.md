# Planetary Modeling Atlas · v15mk2.16

**This is not my data.** None of the maps, gravity models, images or coordinates in this repository were made by me, and I make no claim of ownership over them. They were produced by NASA, ESA, JPL, USGS, the IAU and the researchers and artists credited below. This repository holds reformatted copies of their published work, so that the Planetary Modeling Atlas (a personal, non-commercial viewer) can load it from the web.

## The atlas

`index.html` is the Planetary Modeling Atlas, version **v15mk2.16**, built with Claude. It is v15 (the Solar System map and globes in one view, real lighting and spin, and hand control on a planet's globe) with these added:

- Earth's photo layers: Visible colour, Visible colour with clouds, and Night lights.
- Earth by night: a moonlit blue night side, warm city lights that dim under clouds, an orange band at dusk, and a thin atmosphere at the edge of the photo layers.
- Real time: the page starts at true speed, so Earth spins at its real rate and the clock shows UTC.
- Live data on Earth: satellites placed from CelesTrak orbits, coloured by orbit type (low, medium, geostationary, elliptical) with labelled space stations; click one for its data card and full orbit. Live flights too, with address boxes to choose other sources.
- A closer zoom on each planet's globe (down to 1.1 radii), and the focus view from the Mars Modeling Atlas: click a site or double-click any spot to fly down and look across it at an angle, orbiting around that spot; ← Whole planet goes back.

With GitHub Pages turned on (Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → main, / (root)) it runs at **https://tbirdpower3000.github.io/planetary-atlas-v15mk2/**. Opened there in Chrome, hand control (your camera) and the live data can work, which they can't on claude.ai.

The live layers come straight from their providers in your browser, not from this repository: satellite orbits from [CelesTrak](https://celestrak.org) and aircraft from the [OpenSky Network](https://opensky-network.org) by default. The same "not my data" applies to them. CelesTrak lets any web page read its data; the free flight feeds checked so far (OpenSky, adsb.lol, adsb.fi) don't, so live flights need a small relay before they show on the web page.

- **v15mk2.3**: the focus view from the Mars Modeling Atlas (click a site or double-click a spot to look across it close up; ← Whole planet).

- **v15mk2.7**: the asteroid belt and Jupiter's Trojans on the Solar System map: 6,000 asteroids drawn from the real spread of orbits (with the Kirkwood gaps Jupiter clears), moving on their own Kepler orbits with the map's clock; the Trojans keep about 60° ahead of and behind Jupiter. An Asteroids button turns them off.
- **v15mk2.8**: craters on the Moon and Mars. A Craters switch draws published crater catalogs on the globe as rings to scale (more appear as you zoom in), adds a crater-density layer, and counts the craters in a circle wherever you click or tap: a cumulative size-frequency plot with isochrons and a model age from the Neukum (Moon) or Neukum–Ivanov / Ivanov (Mars) chronologies. The Asteroids button now says on or off.
- **v15mk2.9**: Imagery + relief, a layer on every world that has both a photo map and a topography grid (Earth, the Moon, Mars, and any pack you load): the photo tinted by height, shaded by the relief, or both, with a mix slider; on Earth you can pick which photo, and the night lights still come on after dark.
- **v15mk2.10**: sharper globe and map: each 3D view is now shown at exactly its drawing size in whole pixels. Before, the page layout could leave it a fraction of a pixel larger, so the browser stretched it by one pixel and softened it by up to about 25% (the quality bot caught this after v15mk2.8 and v15mk2.9 made the toolbar taller).
- **v15mk2.11**: Mars through time, from the Mars Planetary Modeling Atlas: the geologic timeline (Wordsworth et al. 2021) with a player that tours the big events; Ancient Mars, the areoid without Tharsis, Hellas and Argyre, with the ancient spin axis and equator; the proposed northern ocean, flooded from the lowest northern point to the Arabia or Deuteronilus shoreline or a level you set, with the Tharsis correction and the mapped shorelines; and the Tharsis, Hellas, Argyre and Borealis outlines and 16 major impacts, appearing as the timeline reaches them. Plus a Sources page for the whole project (`sources.html`), built from every world's data files.
- **v15mk2.12**: the OMEGA mineral maps on Mars (ferric oxides, dust, pyroxene, olivine and near-infrared albedo, Ody et al. 2012) as one Minerals layer with a chooser, grey where OMEGA has no data; and every world's Sources box now says how each source is used, including the features layered on top (craters, live data, Mars through time). The project Sources page shows the same notes for all 122 entries.
- **v15mk2.13**: live flights work. None of the free flight feeds lets another website read it, so the new Live flights job takes a worldwide OpenSky snapshot every few minutes and publishes it as `flights.json` on the `live` branch, which the atlas reads from raw.githubusercontent.com; planes are moved along their heading between snapshots, and the message says how old the snapshot is.
- **v15mk2.14**: tidier world buttons. Each moon sits in one tray with the world it orbits (Earth and the Moon, Mars with Phobos and Deimos, each giant planet with its moons, Pluto and Charon), so the plain-text group labels and the km sizes are gone from the row; a button's size and kind show when you hover over it.
- **v15mk2.15**: your workspace. Everything you load yourself (packs you open, worlds you build, layers and sites you add to a world) is kept together in this browser and comes back next visit, listed under “Your worlds and data” with Open, ⤓ .json and Remove; ⤓ Download workspace saves them all as one file that “Open a planet pack” reads back in any browser. ⤓ Map image saves the layer on the globe as a flat PNG with its colour bar. Zooming in on a planet with moons now lands on its globe only once you are inside the innermost moon's orbit, so you can look around the moons first.
- **v15mk2.16**: hands-free and voice control. 🎤 Voice turns on voice commands: say “Atlas”, then a command (“show Mars”, “next map”, “zoom in”, “brighter”, “sunlight off”, “pause”, “help”); the browser's own speech recognition turns speech into text, on the device where the browser supports it. A Brightness slider for the globe. In hand control, ‹ 🗺 › in the top bar steps through the world's maps and ☀ steps the brightness. 🎯 Adaptive aim watches how steady the cursor is, how often a button takes several tries and whether pinches flicker, adjusts the aim to suit you, and asks whether each change helped (say “good” or “bad”, or pinch 👍 / 👎). Hand control learns each hand separately from which hand the tracker sees, compensates when you sit further from the camera, and checks its own changes against your next presses, undoing any that made things worse. The hand-control training now has its own project, [hand-control-training](https://github.com/tbirdpower3000/hand-control-training) (live at tbirdpower3000.github.io/hand-control-training); the profile it saves is used here automatically, and shared starting settings built from results people choose to share are loaded once enough are in.

## The bots

- `tests/atlas_bot.py` checks that every planet pack opens and decodes, then drives the page: real-time start, landing on Mars and coming back, opening Earth, its photo layers and live-data switches, and no script errors. It also asks each live-data source whether a web page may read it.
- `tests/quality_bot.py` photographs each world in the new version and the one before, at the same frozen moment and camera with labels hidden, and flags anything that got darker, flatter, blurrier or duller, with side-by-side pictures.

Both run on every change, every Monday, and whenever you press **Run workflow** on the Actions tab. Reports are on each run's summary page; pictures are under "Artifacts".

## What was changed

The files were converted by an AI assistant (Claude) into a single JSON format per world. Grids were resampled to 0.25° or 0.5° and stored as 16-bit integers. Gravity maps for Mars, Venus and Mercury were computed from the published spherical-harmonic models with SHTOOLS. Images were resized to 2048 × 1024 pixels, and a few dark ones were brightened for display. These copies are lower in resolution than the originals and were not checked by the original authors. **For any real use, go to the original sources.**

## Format

`index.json` lists the worlds. Each `<id>.json` is one planet pack: gridded maps (int16, base64, rows 90°N→90°S, columns from 0°E), images (equirectangular, 180°W at the left), sites, feature names and sources. The full format is described in the atlas under "Planet pack format".

## Live flights (`live` branch)

`.github/workflows/live-flights.yml` runs `scripts/fetch_flights.py` every 5 minutes (GitHub runs scheduled jobs best-effort, so 5 to 15 minutes apart). It reads OpenSky's anonymous `/states/all` (airplanes.live regional queries as a fallback), keeps the airborne aircraft, and force-pushes a single-commit `live` branch holding `flights.json` (about 1 MB), so the repository doesn't grow. GitHub pauses scheduled jobs in a repository with no activity for 60 days; any push, or Run workflow on the Actions tab, restarts it.

## Sources

`sources.html` lists every dataset, paper and tool behind the atlas, built from the planet packs and the features' own sources by the build.

## Mars through time (`mars-lab.json`)

The ancient-areoid pieces (today, and the parts due to Tharsis, Hellas and Argyre), the ancient pole, Tharsis's deformation (Citron et al. 2018) and the Sholes et al. (2021) shoreline points and the five OMEGA mineral maps (1° cells), as prepared in the Mars Planetary Modeling Atlas. Loaded only when Mars is open.

## Crater catalogs (`craters/`)

Packed for the atlas as int16 latitude×360, int16 longitude×180 and uint16 diameter×20 (base64), largest first; each file carries its citation.

- `craters-mars-robbins2012.json`: Robbins & Hynek (2012), 384,343 craters ≥1 km, from the USGS Astrogeology OGC API (`mars/robbinsv1`).
- `craters-moon-robbins2018.json`: Robbins (2019), 1,296,796 craters ≥1 km, from the USGS Moon Crater Database v1.
- `craters-moon-povilaitis2018.json`: Povilaitis et al. (2018), 22,746 craters 5–20 km (4,425 marked questionable), read from the attribute tables of the six LROC shapefile ZIPs by `scripts/fetch_craters.py`, which runs as the "Fetch crater catalogs" GitHub job. Run it again from the Actions tab to refresh. It also tries Head et al. (2010) from Brown University, whose site was not answering on 3 Nov 2026.

Production and chronology functions are as tabulated in [craterstats](https://github.com/ggmichael/craterstats) (G. Michael, BSD-3).
