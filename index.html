Planet orbits and positions come from JPL's approximate orbital elements (Standish): Table 1 for 1800–2050, and Table 2, with its extra terms for the giant planets, from 3000 BC to AD 3000. JPL gives the Table 2 errors in longitude as about 20″ for Mercury up to 40′ for Uranus. The planets can't be placed this way before 3000 BC: the Solar System is chaotic, so it takes a full numerical integration, and exact positions are lost entirely after tens of millions of years. Dates use the proleptic Gregorian calendar, and 1 BC is followed by AD 1. Pluto, Ceres and Vesta use single-epoch elements, so their positions are approximate, more so far from today. Moon orbits are drawn as circles at their true sizes, tilted by each planet's axial tilt as an approximation of the real planes; their positions along the orbit are illustrative. Body sizes are not to scale.

Live Earth data

Where Earth’s ✈ Live flights and 🛰 Satellites switches get their data. Flights: an address returning OpenSky-style JSON ({"states":[…]}) or readsb-style JSON ({"ac":[…]}, as used by adsb.lol, adsb.fi and airplanes.live). Satellites: an address returning two- or three-line elements (TLE text); {group} becomes the group picked on the globe. Your own copy or a proxy works if it allows cross-site reads (CORS).
