How the planet positions are worked out

Planet orbits and positions come from JPL's approximate orbital elements (Standish): Table 1 for 1800–2050, and Table 2, with its extra terms for the giant planets, from 3000 BC to AD 3000. JPL gives the Table 2 errors in longitude as about 20″ for Mercury up to 40′ for Uranus. The planets can't be placed this way before 3000 BC: the Solar System is chaotic, so it takes a full numerical integration, and exact positions are lost entirely after tens of millions of years. Dates use the proleptic Gregorian calendar, and 1 BC is followed by AD 1. Pluto, Ceres and Vesta use single-epoch elements, so their positions are approximate, more so far from today. Moon orbits are drawn as circles at their true sizes, tilted by each planet's axial tilt as an approximation of the real planes; their positions along the orbit are illustrative. Body sizes are not to scale. The asteroids are a sample of 6,000 drawn from the real spread of the main belt (with the Kirkwood gaps that Jupiter's resonances clear) and of Jupiter's Trojans, which lead and trail Jupiter by about 60°; they show where asteroids are, not where any particular asteroid is.

Crater count

Click or tap a spot on the globe to count the craters around it and estimate the surface's age.

The model age fits the production function's shape to the craters in the chosen size range (a Poisson fit of its height) and reads the age off the chronology function. It assumes one surface of a single age, with no secondary craters, resurfacing or saturation; the plot shows how well that holds. Dashed lines are isochrons for 0.1, 1, 3, 3.5 and 4 billion years.

Ancient Mars · areoid

How Ancient Mars is calculated

Rotation alone would flatten Mars into a smooth bulge; everything else in the areoid comes from lumps of extra or missing mass, so Ancient Mars is measured against that bulge (from the moment of inertia 0.3644, a rotational J2 of 1.820×10⁻³ against the measured 1.957×10⁻³). Tharsis is removed by turning the GMM-3 field into an equivalent surface mass layer, zeroing it inside a cap 50° in radius around the rise (5° N, 250° E) and recomputing the areoid; Hellas and Argyre are removed the same way, following Andrews-Hanna, Zuber & Banerdt (2008). The ancient spin axis is the axis of greatest moment of inertia of the Tharsis-free field, 79.7° N, 198.2° E, a tilt of about 10°, smaller than the 20–25° of Bouley et al. (2016), whose estimate is the second option. Following the timeline, Tharsis's share grows in a straight line from 4.33 to 3.52 Ga and the axis moves with it; Hellas and Argyre switch on at their impact dates. The in-between states are an interpolation, not a measurement.

Water · ancient ocean

Water fills today's MOLA terrain from the lowest point of the northern plains, spilling only where it connects, so Hellas and Argyre stay dry unless the sea reaches their rims. Levels are the mean heights of Carr & Head's (2003) Arabia (−2,287 m) and Deuteronilus (−3,798 m) contacts as compiled by Sholes et al. (2021). With the Tharsis correction the terrain is first restored to before Tharsis loaded the crust (Citron et al. 2018), and the contacts then average −2,611 and −3,706 m. The timeline is schematic: Arabia at or before 4 Ga, Deuteronilus about 3.6 Ga, dry by about 3 Ga (Scheller et al. 2021). Whether these oceans existed is still debated.

    Build from map images

    Use any global map in simple cylindrical (equirectangular) form, twice as wide as tall: a greyscale height map, a colour mosaic, or both.

    Load worlds from the web

    Point this page at a public folder holding index.json and the world files, for example a GitHub repo served through jsDelivr.

    Live Earth data

    Where Earth’s ✈ Live flights and 🛰 Satellites switches get their data. Flights: an address returning OpenSky-style JSON ({"states":[…]}) or readsb-style JSON ({"ac":[…]}, as used by adsb.lol, adsb.fi and airplanes.live). Satellites: an address returning two- or three-line elements (TLE text); {group} becomes the group picked on the globe. Your own copy or a proxy works if it allows cross-site reads (CORS).
